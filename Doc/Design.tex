\section{Executive Design Summary \& Tri-Host Architecture}

This document establishes the electrical, mechanical, and PCB layout specifications for an optimized dual-channel replica of the classic Pete Millett Soundcard Interface. While originally conceived as a single-channel, through-hole audio interface accessory for consumer PC soundcards, this Revision 3.1 architecture transforms the design into a \textbf{high-precision, dual-channel, galvanically isolated active differential probe and wideband signal conditioning front-end}.

The system is engineered to serve three distinct instrumentation hosts interchangeably without hardware reconfiguration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{M-Audio Solo (Consumer 16-Bit USB Audio Interface):} Provides basic single-ended and balanced audio-band spectrum analysis, serving as an isolated, sacrificial signal source and digitizer ($20\,\text{Hz} - 20\,\text{kHz}$, $\approx 92\text{--}94\,\text{dB}$ dynamic range). Because the Solo line input clips near $+2\,\text{dBu}$ ($\approx 1.0\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$), the interface provides a dedicated Low-Clamp jumper position ($\pm 1.4\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$ at the single-ended `SCPROT` node).
    \item \textbf{MOTU M4 (Professional 24-Bit / 192\,kHz Interface):} Connects via dedicated rear-panel balanced Line Inputs 3 \& 4 (+18\,dBu headroom, fixed analog gain) to perform high-resolution automated harmonic distortion (THD+N down to $-110$\,dB), intermodulation, and phase tracking using software suites such as ARTA (STEPS, LIMP), REW, and Multitone.
    \item \textbf{Siglent SDS804X HD (12-Bit High-Resolution Bench Oscilloscope):} Functions as a \textbf{$1,500\,\text{VDC}$ isolated, high-voltage active differential probe}. In High-Resolution (ERES) mode, the oscilloscope achieves 14 to 16 effective bits (ENOB) with continuous bandwidth from $3.35\,\text{Hz}$ past $2.0\,\text{MHz}$, making it ideal for inspecting ultrasonic oscillation, power-supply rail ripple, slew-rate transients, and Class-D amplifier switching noise without ground-loop hazards.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key Design Modifications from Baseline}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Dual-Channel Topology:} Complete mirrored duplication of attenuators, fusible limiters, OPA1656 buffers, INA134 difference stages, clamping bridges, and DRV135 drivers for stereo operation.
    \item \textbf{Power Switching \& Kick-Back Protection:} Added a primary Master Switch ($SW_{102}$) and secondary Dual-Rail Switch ($SW_{101}$), a 3-LED status monitoring network, and dual secondary SMAJ16A TVS clamping diodes ($D_{105}, D_{106}$) across the $\pm 15\,\text{V}$ rails to absorb kickback current during high-voltage input faults.
    \item \textbf{Chassis Ground Isolation Lug:} Integrated a dedicated chassis bonding network comprising a $1\,\text{M}\Omega$ $1\,\text{W}$ bleeder resistor in parallel with a $10\,\text{nF}$ $1\,\text{kV}$ ceramic capacitor connecting chassis earth to isolated analog ground for RF shielding and electrostatic drainage without ground loops.
    \item \textbf{Transition to Vishay NFR25 Fusible Resistors:} Replaced both Pete's original noisy $50\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistors and intermediate depletion FET concepts with socketed $4.7\,\text{k}\Omega$ flameproof fusible resistors ($R_{312}, R_{313}$), lowering thermal noise to $8.82\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ while ensuring safe open-circuit disconnection under catastrophic fault conditions.
    \item \textbf{BAV199 Low-Leakage Diodes:} Replaced legacy 1N4148 diodes with BAV199 dual series pairs ($I_R \le 5\,\text{pA}$, $C_d \le 1.5\,\text{pF}$) backed by SMAJ16A TVS power-rail clamps.
    \item \textbf{Switch-Mounted 0.6\,W Attenuator Ladder:} Re-engineered the precision divider ladder ($90.9\,\text{k}\Omega$, $9.09\,\text{k}\Omega$, $909\,\Omega$) to solder directly across the rotary switch solder lugs in a radial halo, completely eliminating high-frequency wire-to-wire crosstalk.
    \item \textbf{3-Wire Guarded PTFE Interconnect:} Replaced lossy coaxial cables with 3 unshielded PTFE wires routed to an interleaved 5-pin header, providing $>5.08\,\text{mm}$ high-voltage creepage and grounded electrostatic shielding.
    \item \textbf{Buffer Loop Optimization (2\,MHz):} Sized OPA1656 feedback capacitors to $4.7\,\text{pF}$ C0G/NP0 ($C_{308}, C_{309}$) with spare parallel DNP pads, pushing the loop transmission pole to $4.51\,\text{MHz}$.
    \item \textbf{Elimination of Input Ground Switch (SW5) \& Monitor Switch (SW3):} Hardwired as a permanently floating differential front-end to eliminate the risk of accidental short-circuits to Mains Earth Ground when paired with a bench oscilloscope.
    \item \textbf{Elimination of DC Zero-Adjust Potentiometer ($R_{25}$):} Tied INA134 Pin 1 directly to local isolated analog ground (AGND), preserving $>90\,\text{dB}$ CMRR.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Level Off-Signal Zener Clamping Bridge:} Relocated voltage-defining Zeners into DC bias legs selecting between $\pm 1.4\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$ (Solo protection) and $\pm 5.4\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$ (M4 line level / Scope dynamic range) without placing non-linear Zener capacitance across the audio signal path.
    \item \textbf{Universal Output Connectivity:} Simultaneous balanced $1/4''$ TRS jacks (via DRV135, $G=2$) and unbalanced BNC jacks (via $200\,\Omega$ isolation resistor $R_{311}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the Oscilloscope Cannot Fully Replace the Soundcard}
While the SDS804X HD in High-Res mode achieves an effective resolution of $\approx 15$\,bits with wide bandwidth, it cannot replace a dedicated audio interface for precision distortion testing:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Delta-Sigma Noise Shaping:} Audio converters (such as the ESS Sabre32 in the M4) oversample and push quantization noise outside the audio band, yielding an ultra-quiet floor down to $-115\text{ to } -130\,\text{dBV}$.
    \item \textbf{Wideband Johnson Noise:} The oscilloscope front-end must maintain megahertz flat bandwidth. Even when band-limited, wideband thermal noise keeps the scope's noise floor near $-88\,\text{dB}$. Op-amp or amplifier harmonics sitting at $0.0003\%$ ($-110\,\text{dB}$) disappear entirely beneath the scope's quantization floor.
    \item \textbf{Software Sweep Automation:} ARTA (STEPS) and REW automate multi-point stepped-sine distortion vs.\ frequency and power sweeps, automatically tracking harmonics HD2 through HD10. Oscilloscopes require manual data export and post-processing for similar audio metrics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the Oscilloscope Requires This Active Front-End}
Conversely, connecting an oscilloscope directly to an audio circuit via a standard 10X passive probe presents severe limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{The Mains Earth Short Hazard:} The outer shield of the scope's BNC jack is hardwired to Mains Earth. Clipping a probe ground lead to a floating tube cathode resistor, a bridge-tied load (BTL) amplifier output, or an inverter rail creates a dead short to earth ground, instantly destroying circuit traces or the scope probe. The interface's $1,500\,\text{VDC}$ galvanic isolation completely eliminates this hazard.
    \item \textbf{High DC Bias Dynamic Range Loss:} Probing a $300\,\text{VDC}$ vacuum tube plate carrying a $100\,\text{mVAC}$ signal forces a standard scope to a $50\,\text{V}/\text{div}$ or $100\,\text{V}/\text{div}$ scale. The $100\,\text{mV}$ audio ripple occupies a fraction of a single ADC quantization level. The interface's $400\,\text{V}$ polypropylene film capacitors block the DC component completely, allowing the scope to operate on its most sensitive $20\,\text{mV}/\text{div}$ scale.
    \item \textbf{Capacitive Loading on High-Z Nodes:} A 10X passive probe has $\approx 15\,\text{pF}$ of tip capacitance, which at $20\,\text{kHz}$ represents an impedance of only $530\,\text{k}\Omega$. On a high-impedance tube plate ($100\,\text{k}\Omega$), this causes high-frequency roll-off and phase shift. The interface's OPA1656 CMOS inputs buffer the node with negligible capacitance and $10^{13}\,\Omega$ input resistance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with DC Telemetry Front-End (TI INA228)}
A dedicated DC telemetry circuit based on the Texas Instruments INA228 serves a fundamentally different measurement role than this wideband AC-coupled analog front-end:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Signal Domain:} The INA228 is an integrated 20-bit $\Delta\Sigma$ digital power/energy monitor strictly dedicated to DC bus voltage, shunt current, and slow thermal telemetry. This interface is an ultra-low-distortion, dynamic AC front-end with analog bandwidth extending to $2.5\,\text{MHz}$.
    \item \textbf{Common-Mode Ceiling:} The INA228 is restricted to an absolute maximum common-mode bus voltage of $+85\,\text{VDC}$. This AFE withstands continuous $150\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$ ($212\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$) and intermittent $250\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$ ($353\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$), with a dielectric isolation barrier of $1,500\,\text{VDC}$ between device-under-test and the host chassis.
    \item \textbf{Throughput \& Interface:} The INA228 digitizes internally and outputs over an $\text{I}^2\text{C}$ digital bus at conversion rates up to $50\,\text{kSPS}$. This interface provides real-time, zero-latency continuous analog waveforms to audio analyzers and high-speed bench oscilloscopes.
\end{itemize}

\section{DC Power Supply, Rail Clamping \& Enclosure Grounding}

The power delivery network converts an unregulated $+5\,\text{VDC}$ supply into low-noise, fully isolated $\pm 15\,\text{VDC}$ analog split rails. The schematic architecture integrates dual-stage power switching, multi-LED status reporting, TVS rail protection against clamping kickback, and an AC-coupled chassis ground lug.

\subsection{Power Input Connector \& Dual-Pole Master Switch \texorpdfstring{($SW_{102}$)}{(SW102)}}
Power enters via a dedicated 6-pin USB-C receptacle (Power Only, J101):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Connector Pinout \& CC Logic:} Provides two $V_{\text{BUS}}$ power pins, two Ground pins, and dual Configuration Channel (CC1/CC2) pins, each terminated to ground through an independent $5.1\,\text{k}\Omega \pm 1\%$ pulldown resistor.
    \item \textbf{Master Power Switch ($SW_{102}$):} A DPST toggle switch ($SW_{102\text{A}}, SW_{102\text{B}}$) placed immediately at the USB-C power entry point. This completely disconnects both the $+5\,\text{V}_{\text{in}}$ supply rail and the primary ground reference (\texttt{GNDREF}) when open, preventing quiescent drain and USB host back-powering.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Primary Crowbar Protection \texorpdfstring{($F_{101}, D_{101}$)}{(F101, D101)}}
Upstream of the DC-DC converter, an explicit crowbar stage guards against overvoltage transients from off-spec USB adapters:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Series Overcurrent Limiter ($F_{101}$):} An 1812 surface-mount resettable PTC fuse (\textbf{Bourns MF-MSMF110}, $I_{\text{hold}} = 1.1\,\text{A}$, $I_{\text{trip}} = 2.2\,\text{A}$, $V_{\text{max}} \ge 16\,\text{V}$). The 1812 footprint provides low internal resistance ($< 0.1\,\Omega$) and high heat dissipation.
    \item \textbf{Shunt Clamping Diode ($D_{101}$):} An SMD DO-214AB (SMC) Transient Voltage Suppressor, specifically the \textbf{SMCJ6.0A} ($V_{\text{RWM}} = 6.0\,\text{V}$, $V_{\text{BR}} = 6.67\,\text{V}$ to $7.37\,\text{V}$, $1500\,\text{W}$ peak pulse power). A $6.0\,\text{V}$ standoff diode clamps immediately above nominal $5\,\text{V}$, driving $F_{101}$ into high-impedance latch-off within milliseconds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Isolated DC-DC Converter \texorpdfstring{($U_{101}$)}{(U101)} \& Analog Rail Switch \texorpdfstring{($SW_{101}$)}{(SW101)}}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{XP Power JCA0305D03 ($U_{101}$):} $3\,\text{W}$ continuous rating, nominal $+5\,\text{VDC}$ input ($4.5\,\text{V}$ to $9.0\,\text{V}$ operating window), fully regulated $\pm 15\,\text{VDC}$ dual output at $\pm 100\,\text{mA}$.
    \item \textbf{Galvanic Isolation:} $1,500\,\text{VDC}$ baseline isolation breaks ground continuity between the computer sound card and floating device-under-test (DUT) circuits.
    \item \textbf{EMI/RFI Containment:} Housed in an integrated nickel-coated copper metal enclosure acting as an intrinsic Faraday shield.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Rail Filtering ($C_{103}$--$C_{106}$):} The isolated $+15\,\text{V}$ and $-15\,\text{V}$ outputs are filtered by $330\,\mu\text{F}$ $25\,\text{V}$ low-ESR radial electrolytics ($C_{103}, C_{104}$) and local $0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ X7R ceramic bypass caps ($C_{105}, C_{106}$).
    \item \textbf{Main Power Switch ($SW_{101}$):} A DPDT switch ($SW_{101\text{A}}, SW_{101\text{B}}$) isolates downstream analog circuitry (\texttt{+VDC}, \texttt{-VDC}) from the DC-DC output, allowing commissioning and power-rail verification without powering active ICs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3-LED Diagnostic Monitoring Subsystem}
Three dedicated status LEDs provide instantaneous visual verification of system power integrity:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Master Power LED ($D_{102}$, \texttt{M\_PWR}):} Positioned on the primary $5\,\text{V}$ side after $SW_{102}$, powered via series resistor $R_{101}$ ($470\,\Omega$):
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{LED,pri}} = \frac{5.0\,\text{V} - 2.0\,\text{V}}{470\,\Omega} \approx \mathbf{6.38\,\text{mA}}
    \end{equation}
    Indicates that USB-C power is valid and the master switch is closed.
    \item \textbf{Positive Analog Rail LED ($D_{103}$, \texttt{PWR\_PS}):} Connected between the switched $+15\,\text{VDC}$ rail and isolated analog ground via $R_{102}$ ($3.0\,\text{k}\Omega$):
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{LED,pos}} = \frac{15.0\,\text{V} - 2.0\,\text{V}}{3{,}000\,\Omega} \approx \mathbf{4.33\,\text{mA}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Negative Analog Rail LED ($D_{104}$, \texttt{PWR\_NEG}):} Connected between isolated analog ground and the $-15\,\text{VDC}$ rail via $R_{103}$ ($3.0\,\text{k}\Omega$), sinking $\approx 4.33\,\text{mA}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Secondary TVS Rail Clamping Kickback Protection \texorpdfstring{($D_{105}, D_{106}$)}{(D105, D106)}}
During an extreme high-voltage input fault (e.g., $250\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$ intermittent), fault current shunted through the BAV199 input protection diodes flows directly into the $+15\,\text{V}$ and $-15\,\text{V}$ analog power rails. 

\textbf{The Rail Pump-Up Hazard:} The XP Power JCA0305D03 is a unidirectional boost-buck flyback converter; its synchronous output rectifiers can only \textit{source} current into the load. They cannot sink reverse current back into the transformer. Injected fault current ($\approx 72\,\text{mA}_{\text{pk}}$) charges filter capacitors $C_{103}$ and $C_{104}$, causing the supply rails to rapidly pump upward above $\pm 20\,\text{V}$, destroying the OPA1656 and INA134 op-amps (absolute maximum rating $\pm 18.0\,\text{V}$).

\textbf{The Solution:} Two \textbf{Littelfuse SMAJ16A} surface-mount TVS diodes ($D_{105}, D_{106}$) clamp the rails:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Positive Rail Clamp ($D_{105}$):} Cathode tied to $+15\,\text{VDC}$, anode tied to isolated GND. Reverse standoff voltage $V_{\text{RWM}} = 16.0\,\text{V}$; breakdown $V_{\text{BR}} = 17.8\,\text{V}$ to $19.7\,\text{V}$.
    \item \textbf{Negative Rail Clamp ($D_{106}$):} Cathode tied to isolated GND, anode tied to $-15\,\text{VDC}$.
    \item \textbf{Operation:} Under normal conditions ($|\pm 15.0\,\text{V}|$), both diodes remain non-conducting with leakage current $< 1\,\mu\text{A}$. \end{itemize}

\section{Analog Front-End (AFE) Signal Conditioning \& Protection}

\subsection{High-Voltage DC Blocking Capacitors (C302, C303, C402, C403)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Capacitance Optimization:} Sized at $0.47\,\mu\text{F}$ with a $400\,\text{VDC}$ working rating.
    \item \textbf{Cut-off Calculation:} Across the $101.0\,\text{k}\Omega$ system input impedance, the high-pass $-3\,\text{dB}$ corner frequency is:
    \begin{equation}
        f_c = \frac{1}{2 \pi R C} = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 101{,}000 \times (0.47 \times 10^{-6})} \approx 3.35\,\text{Hz}
    \end{equation}
    At $20\,\text{Hz}$, the magnitude attenuation is below $0.06\,\text{dB}$, and phase shift is negligible.
    \item \textbf{Dielectric Selection:} Metallized Polypropylene (PP) Film (e.g., WIMA MKP4 or Panasonic ECQ-E). High-voltage Class II SMD ceramic capacitors (X7R) are strictly prohibited due to severe Voltage Coefficient of Capacitance (VCC) non-linearities.
    \item \textbf{CAD Footprint Provisioning:} Multi-pitch radial through-hole pads with concentric drill points spaced at $5.0\,\text{mm}$, $7.5\,\text{mm}$, $10.0\,\text{mm}$, and $15.0\,\text{mm}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Protection Limiting: The \texorpdfstring{$4.7\,\text{k}\Omega$}{4.7 k-ohm} Fusible Resistor}
Earlier design revisions considered back-to-back Microchip LND150 depletion MOSFETs to provide current limiting. While attractive for low-frequency current pinching, the LND150 introduces severe limitations in a wideband active probe:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Non-Linear Junction Capacitance:} The LND150 exhibits an input capacitance ($C_{\text{iss}}$) of $15\text{ to }30\,\text{pF}$ that varies significantly with instantaneous terminal voltage. Across a $100\,\text{k}\Omega$ divider impedance, this voltage-dependent capacitance produces harmonic distortion and limits bandwidth below $500\,\text{kHz}$.
    \item \textbf{Channel Resistance Modulation:} As signal swings exceed several volts, the drain-to-source channel dynamic resistance modulates prior to pinch-off, increasing measured distortion by over $18\,\text{dB}$ at $10\,\text{kHz}$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The Fusible Resistor Solution ($R_{312}, R_{313}$):} The LND150 pair is replaced with a single socketed \textbf{Vishay NFR25 $4.7\,\text{k}\Omega$ flameproof fusible resistor}.

\subsubsection{Johnson Thermal Noise Analysis}
The thermal noise density generated by a resistor is given by:
\begin{equation}
    e_n = \sqrt{4 k_B T R}
\end{equation}
At $T = 300\,\text{K}$ with $R = 4.7\,\text{k}\Omega$:
\begin{equation}
    e_{n(4.7\text{k})} = \sqrt{4 \times (1.38 \times 10^{-23}) \times 300 \times 4{,}700} \approx \mathbf{8.82\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}}
\end{equation}
Root-sum-squaring this value with the OPA1656 input voltage noise ($2.9\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$):
\begin{equation}
    e_{n(\text{total})} = \sqrt{8.82^2 + 2.9^2} \approx \mathbf{9.28\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}}
\end{equation}
In a standard $20\,\text{kHz}$ audio bandwidth, the total integrated input noise is:
\begin{equation}
    V_{n,\text{audio}} = 9.28\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}} \times \sqrt{20{,}000} \approx 1.31\,\mu\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}
\end{equation}
Referred to a nominal $2.0\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$ line level, this maintains an input signal-to-noise ratio of \textbf{$123.6\,\text{dB}$}, preserving the full dynamic range of the MOTU M4 ADC.

\subsubsection{Overvoltage Fault Current \& Air-Gap PCB Isolation}
During a maximum intermittent $250\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$ ($353\,\text{V}_{\text{pk}}$) fault, the peak current shunted into the rail diodes is:
\begin{equation}
    I_{\text{fault,pk}} = \frac{353\,\text{V} - 15.6\,\text{V}}{4{,}700\,\Omega} \approx \mathbf{71.8\,\text{mA}}
\end{equation}
The continuous RMS fault dissipation is:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{fault}} = \frac{(250\,\text{V})^2}{4{,}700\,\Omega} \approx \mathbf{13.3\,\text{W}}
\end{equation}
Under this sustained overload, the Vishay NFR25 fuses cleanly within 2 seconds without flame, explosive fragmentation, or carbon tracking. 

\textbf{PCB Creepage Slot:} To prevent high-voltage arcing across carbonized FR-4 substrate during a fusing event, a \textbf{$1.5\,\text{mm} \times 6.0\,\text{mm}$ isolation slot} is routed through the PCB (\textsf{Edge.Cuts}) directly beneath the body of $R_{312}$ and $R_{313}$. Sockets are implemented using Mill-Max gold-plated receptacle pins to allow rapid replacement after a fault.

\subsection{Diode Selection: Upgrade from 1N4148 to BAV199}
Pete Millett's original design specified standard 1N4148 diodes for rail clamping. These are replaced with \textbf{Nexperia BAV199} dual diodes in SOT-23 packages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Reverse Leakage Current ($I_R$):} A standard 1N4148 leaks $25\text{ to }50\,\text{nA}$ at room temperature, increasing to $>5\,\mu\text{A}$ at $75^\circ\text{C}$. When flowing through a high-impedance divider node, this leakage creates significant non-linear DC offsets and measurable odd-order harmonic distortion. The BAV199 guarantees a maximum leakage of \textbf{$5\,\text{pA}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$} ($<5\,\text{nA}$ at $150^\circ\text{C}$), completely eliminating leakage-induced distortion.
    \item \textbf{Junction Capacitance ($C_j$):} The BAV199 maintains $C_j \le 1.5\,\text{pF}$ under reverse bias, preserving phase margin and high-frequency linearity out to $2.5\,\text{MHz}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the Input Attenuator Ladder Must Be Retained}
It is tempting to consider bypassing the input resistor divider when using an oscilloscope host, since bench scopes feature their own internal vertical attenuators. This is fundamentally prohibited:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Probing a high-voltage circuit (such as a power amplifier output or tube plate swinging $150\,\text{V}$ to $250\,\text{V}$) without attenuation would drive the OPA1656 inputs far past their $\pm 15\,\text{V}$ rails. The BAV199 clamping diodes would conduct continuously, shunting heavy fault current and severely clipping the waveform into a distorted square wave at $\pm 15.6\,\text{V}$.
    \item The precision stepped attenuator ($1\times, 10\times, 100\times, 1000\times$ ranges) scales high-voltage potentials down to the linear operating region of the op-amp buffers, ensuring the buffer and instrumentation amplifier operate within their linear dynamic range.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Frequency Compensation Capacitors Are Not Required}
Standard oscilloscope passive probes ($10\times, 100\times$) use $9\,\text{M}\Omega$ divider ladders. In those circuits, a $15\,\text{pF}$ input capacitance creates an uncompensated pole at $\approx 1.1\,\text{kHz}$, necessitating adjustable trimmer capacitors across every range.

In this active interface, the entire attenuator ladder is scaled down to \textbf{$101.0\,\text{k}\Omega$ total resistance} ($90.9\,\text{k}\Omega + 9.09\,\text{k}\Omega + 909\,\Omega + 102\,\Omega$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{$1\times$ Range (Direct Buffer):} No resistive division; buffer input capacitance ($\approx 6\,\text{pF}$ total) is driven directly by the low-impedance source.
    \item \textbf{$10\times$ Range (Worst-Case Divider Impedance):}
    \begin{equation}
        R_{\text{th}} = 90.9\,\text{k}\Omega \parallel (9.09\,\text{k}\Omega + 909\,\Omega + 102\,\Omega) \approx \mathbf{9.09\,\text{k}\Omega}
    \end{equation}
    The total node capacitance (OPA1656 $C_{\text{in}} = 3\,\text{pF}$, BAV199 $C_j = 1.5\,\text{pF}$, stray PCB $C_s \approx 1.5\,\text{pF}$) is $\approx 6.0\,\text{pF}$. The natural uncompensated high-frequency pole is:
    \begin{equation}
        f_{-3\text{dB}} = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 9{,}090\,\Omega \times 6.0 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F}} \approx \mathbf{2.92\,\text{MHz}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{$100\times$ Range:} $R_{\text{th}} \approx 1.0\,\text{k}\Omega \implies f_{-3\text{dB}} \approx \mathbf{26.5\,\text{MHz}}$.
    \item \textbf{$1000\times$ Range:} $R_{\text{th}} \approx 102\,\Omega \implies f_{-3\text{dB}} \approx \mathbf{260\,\text{MHz}}$.
\end{itemize}
Because the worst-case pole sits comfortably above $2.0\,\text{MHz}$, frequency compensation capacitors across the ladder resistors are unnecessary. This eliminates phase-trim calibration procedures.

\subsection{Why 5-Wire Bundles and Shielded Coax Fail at 2 MHz}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Failure of 5 Flying Wires:} Routing 5 unshielded wires from the switch back to the PCB creates mutual stray capacitances ($5\text{ to }15\,\text{pF}$) between adjacent leads. High-voltage signals on the $1\times$ lead capacitively bypass the divider ladder and couple directly into the $100\times$ and $1000\times$ taps.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Shielded Coax (RG-178):} Standard RG-178 cable exhibits a capacitance of $\approx 95\,\text{pF/m}$ ($2.4\,\text{pF/inch}$). A 3-inch run with grounded shield adds $\approx 8\text{ to }10\,\text{pF}$ of shunt capacitance to ground. For the $10\times$ tap ($R_{\text{th}} = 9.09\,\text{k}\Omega$), this rolls off the frequency response at:
    \begin{equation}
        f_{-3\text{dB}} = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 9{,}090\,\Omega \times 16 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F}} \approx \mathbf{1.09\,\text{MHz}}
    \end{equation}
    Grounded coaxial cable degrades bandwidth well below the $2.0\,\text{MHz}$ probe requirement.
\end{itemize}

\textit{DC Offset Analysis:} Across the worst-case $100\,\text{k}\Omega$ input resistance, a traditional bipolar op-amp (NE5532, $I_B \approx 72\,\text{nA}$) develops an uncalibrated DC offset:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{offset}} = I_B \times R_{\text{in}} = 72 \times 10^{-9}\,\text{A} \times 100 \times 10^3\,\Omega = 7.2\,\text{mV}
\end{equation}
In contrast, the OPA1656 generates:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{offset}} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{A} \times 100 \times 10^3\,\Omega = 0.001\,\text{mV} \quad (1.0\,\mu\text{V})
\end{equation}
This negligible DC offset preserves high DC stability without requiring manual calibration.

\subsection{OPA1656 Feedback Optimization for 2 MHz Flatness}
The OPA1656 buffer amplifiers operate with local feedback networks ($R_{309}, R_{310} = 7.5\,\text{k}\Omega$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Prior designs utilized $10\text{ to }22\,\text{pF}$ feedback capacitors, causing bandwidth to roll off at $964\,\text{kHz}$ and degrading performance as a $2\,\text{MHz}$ scope probe.
    \item In this revision, feedback capacitors ($C_{308}, C_{309}$) are set to \textbf{$4.7\,\text{pF}$ C0G/NP0}.
    \item The high-frequency feedback pole is shifted to:
    \begin{equation}
        f_{\text{fb}} = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 7{,}500\,\Omega \times 4.7 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F}} \approx \mathbf{4.51\,\text{MHz}}
    \end{equation}
    This phase-lead zero stabilizes the buffer loop against board parasitics while maintaining flat frequency response (within $-0.3\,\text{dB}$) at $2.0\,\text{MHz}$. Unpopulated 0805 parallel pads ($C_{314}, C_{313}$) are provided for fine-tuning during testing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Elimination of the DC Nulling Network \texorpdfstring{($R_{25}$)}{(R25)}}
In the original design, a $100\,\text{k}\Omega$ cermet potentiometer ($R_{25}$) was connected to the reference pin (Pin 1) of the INA134 difference amplifier to null DC offsets. 

\textbf{Engineering Justification for Elimination:}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Preservation of CMRR:} The Texas Instruments INA134 relies on four internal, on-chip thin-film resistors laser-trimmed to an absolute ratio match better than $0.005\%$. This precise matching guarantees a Common-Mode Rejection Ratio of $>90\,\text{dB}$ across the audio band. Injecting an external DC voltage into Pin 1 introduces source impedance non-linearities and temperature coefficients that unbalance the internal bridge, degrading CMRR by $15\,\text{dB}$ to $25\,\text{dB}$.
    \item \textbf{CMOS Buffer Zero Drift:} The OPA1656 features an input bias current of only $10\,\text{pA}$, reducing offset drift to negligible microvolt levels.
    \item \textbf{Hardwired Ground Implementation:} Pin 1 of U303 and U403 is hardwired directly to the solid analog ground plane (AGND). Both target audio interfaces and oscilloscopes support software AC coupling, rendering sub-millivolt DC offsets irrelevant.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Removal of Grounding Switch (SW5) \& Monitor Switch (SW3)}
Pete Millett's input section included `SW5` (which shunted the negative input $V_{\text{INN}}$ directly to local chassis ground) and `SW3` (which looped the signal generator back into the input). Both switches have been eliminated:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Ground Loop Hazard with Scopes:} Connecting the interface output to the Siglent SDS804X HD references local analog ground to Mains Earth Ground via the scope BNC shield. If `SW5` were closed while probing a non-zero circuit potential (such as a tube cathode bias network or Class-D bridge output), it would create a \textbf{direct short-circuit to Mains Earth}, vaporizing traces or damaging the device under test.
    \item \textbf{Permanent Floating Differential Operation:} By completely removing `SW5`, $V_{\text{INN}}$ is routed through DC blocking capacitor $C_{303}$ into the non-inverting input of buffer U302B. The front-end remains permanently floating and differential, providing complete galvanic safety regardless of switch settings.
\end{itemize}

% SECTION 7: HIGH-FREQUENCY STABILITY & PCB LAYOUT
% =========================================================================
\section{High-Frequency Stability, Layout \& Oscillation Prevention}

The Texas Instruments OPA1656 is a wide-bandwidth operational amplifier ($f_{\text{unity}} = 53\,\text{MHz}$). At these frequencies, parasitic board capacitances and load cable capacitances can erode amplifier phase margin, resulting in high-frequency ringing or sustained oscillation.

\subsection{Active Layout Stability Rules}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Direct Rail Decoupling:} High-frequency surface-mount ceramic bypass capacitors ($0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ X7R, 0805) are placed directly against Pin 8 ($V+$) and Pin 4 ($V-$). Power traces route through the capacitor solder pad prior to entering the IC lead frame. A $10\,\mu\text{F}$ tantalum or low-ESR electrolytic capacitor is placed within $10\,\text{mm}$ upstream on each rail.
    \item \textbf{Feedback Phase Compensation ($C_{\text{fb}}$):} An 0805 capacitor footprint is routed in parallel with the main feedback resistor ($R_{\text{fb}}$). This footprint is populated with a $4.7\,\text{pF}$ C0G/NP0 dielectric ceramic capacitor ($C_{308}, C_{309}$).
    \item \textbf{Series Output Isolation ($R_{311}$):} Driving capacitive coaxial cables directly from low-impedance op-amp outputs degrades phase margin according to $f_{\text{pole}} = \frac{1}{2 \pi R_o C_{\text{load}}}$. A dedicated $200\,\Omega$ thin-film resistor ($R_{311}$) isolates the INA134 output from cable capacitance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{PCB Floorplan \& Partitioning Strategy}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Galvanic Isolation Barrier:} A strict $4.0\,\text{mm}$ clearance moat separates the non-isolated USB-C $5\,\text{V}$ input domain from the isolated $\pm 15\,\text{V}$ analog domain. No copper traces, ground planes, or component pads bridge this barrier except for the footprint of the JCA0305D03 module.
    \item \textbf{Mirrored Symmetry:} Channel 1 and Channel 2 analog signal paths are routed with identical trace lengths and parasitic capacitances, placed symmetrically north and south of a central analog ground bus.
    \item \textbf{Isolated Ground Plane (AGND):} The secondary side of the board utilizes an unbroken, contiguous copper pour on Layer 2 dedicated exclusively to isolated analog ground. This provides low-impedance return paths and minimizes channel-to-channel crosstalk to below $-110\,\text{dB}$ at $1\,\text{kHz}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Zener Diodes Cannot Be Placed in the Signal Path}
When designing an overvoltage protection clamp for an audio measurement system, clamping diodes cannot simply be connected across the audio line:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Junction Capacitance Non-Linearity:} Standard Zener diodes (e.g., 4.7\,V Zeners) exhibit massive junction capacitances ($C_j \approx 100\,\text{pF}$ to $400\,\text{pF}$). This capacitance varies non-linearly with applied signal voltage, generating significant harmonic distortion (THD).
    \item \textbf{Soft Reverse Breakdown Knee:} Zener diodes begin conducting microamps of leakage current well below their nominal breakdown rating. On audio peaks above $1\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$, this leakage current clips signal peaks roundedly, introducing odd-order harmonics into measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Clamping Threshold Calculations}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Position 1 (M-Audio Solo Mode):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Jumper \texttt{JP301}/\texttt{JP302} routes bias nodes to forward-biased diodes ($V_f \approx 0.70\,\text{V}$).
        \item Node A is clamped at $+0.70\,\text{V}$; Node B is clamped at $-0.70\,\text{V}$.
        \item Symmetrical signal clipping occurs at:
        \begin{equation}
            V_{\text{clamp}} = \pm(V_{\text{bias}} + V_{f(\text{steering})}) = \pm(0.70\,\text{V} + 0.70\,\text{V}) = \mathbf{\pm 1.40\,\text{V}_{\text{peak}}}
        \end{equation}
        This limits differential output to $\approx 1.0\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$, protecting the 16-bit M-Audio Solo front-end from digital clipping.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Position 2 (MOTU M4 \& Oscilloscope Mode):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The bias nodes connect through \textbf{1SMB5917} $4.7\,\text{V}$ Zener diodes ($DZ_{301}, DZ_{302}$).
        \item Node A sits at $+4.70\,\text{V}$; Node B sits at $-4.70\,\text{V}$.
        \item Symmetrical signal clipping occurs at:
        \begin{equation}
            V_{\text{clamp}} = \pm(4.70\,\text{V} + 0.70\,\text{V}) = \mathbf{\pm 5.40\,\text{V}_{\text{peak}}} \quad (\mathbf{\approx 3.82\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}})
        \end{equation}
        This takes full advantage of the MOTU M4's $+18\,\text{dBu}$ ($6.15\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$) line input headroom while protecting the converter.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Bias Resistor Sizing and Noise Suppression}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Bias Current:} With $4.7\,\text{V}$ Zeners active on $\pm 15\,\text{V}$ rails:
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{bias}} = \frac{15.0\,\text{V} - 4.70\,\text{V}}{1{,}000\,\Omega} = \mathbf{10.3\,\text{mA}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Power Dissipation:} Resistors $R_{301}$ and $R_{307}$ dissipate $(10.3\,\text{mA})^2 \times 1\,\text{k}\Omega = 106\,\text{mW}$ ($0.25\,\text{W}$ rated). Zener power is $4.7\,\text{V} \times 10.3\,\text{mA} = 48.4\,\text{mW}$, well below the 1.5\,W rating of the 1SMB5917.
    \item \textbf{Avalanche Shot Noise Shunting:} Zener diodes generate wideband avalanche noise. Capacitors $C_{301}$ and $C_{304}$ ($0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ X7R ceramic) are placed directly in parallel with each Zener to shunt shot noise to AGND.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Universal Dual Output Stage: DRV135 \& Dedicated Scope BNC Tap}
Both channels feature two parallel, simultaneously active output connections:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Unbalanced BNC Jack (`J303`, `SCPROT`):} Directly tapped from the output of the INA134 difference amplifier through series isolation resistor $R_{311}$ ($200\,\Omega$ thin film). When connected to an oscilloscope with input termination set to $1\,\text{M}\Omega$, loading attenuation is negligible:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Attenuation} = \frac{1{,}000{,}000\,\Omega}{1{,}000{,}000\,\Omega + 200\,\Omega} = 0.9998 \quad (-\mathbf{0.0017\,\text{dB}})
    \end{equation}
    This feeds directly into the Siglent SDS804X HD oscilloscope or unbalanced line-in soundcards.
    \item \textbf{Balanced $1/4''$ TRS Jack (`J302`):} Driven by a Texas Instruments \textbf{DRV135UA} balanced line driver (SOIC-8). The DRV135 provides an exact laser-trimmed differential gain of $2\times$ ($+6\,\text{dB}$), driving long balanced shielded cables into the MOTU M4 rear Line Inputs 3 \& 4 with high common-mode ground noise rejection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bench Commissioning \& Verification Workflow}

Once fabricated, the dual-channel interface should be validated following this step-by-step procedure:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Cold Resistance \& Isolation Verification:} With power disconnected and $SW_{102}$ open, verify $>100\,\text{M}\Omega$ insulation resistance between the USB-C shield and the analog BNC shells using a high-voltage insulation tester or high-range DMM. Ensure Pin 1 of both INA134 ICs measures $< 0.1\,\Omega$ to isolated AGND. Verify that $J_{102}$ measures $1.0\,\text{M}\Omega$ to isolated AGND.
    \item \textbf{Power Rail Check:} Close Master Switch $SW_{102}$ while keeping Secondary Switch $SW_{101}$ open. Verify LED $D_{102}$ illuminates. Check test points TP101 and TP102 relative to TP103: rails must measure precisely between $\pm 14.85\,\text{V}$ and $\pm 15.15\,\text{V}$ with under $5\,\text{mV}_{\text{p-p}}$ switching ripple on the oscilloscope. Close $SW_{101}$ and verify LEDs $D_{103}$ and $D_{104}$ illuminate symmetrically.
    \item \textbf{Output Clamping Calibration:} 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Set JP301/JP302 to Low (Solo Mode). Inject a $1\,\text{kHz}$ sine wave into the front BNC and increase generator level. Observe on the SDS804X HD that the waveform begins symmetrical hard clipping at precisely $\pm 1.40\,\text{V}_{\text{peak}} \pm 0.05\,\text{V}$.
        \item Set JP301/JP302 to High (M4/Scope Mode). Verify that the clipping threshold shifts upward to $\pm 5.40\,\text{V}_{\text{peak}} \pm 0.15\,\text{V}$ without distortion on signals below $3.5\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Host Connection Protocols:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{M-Audio Solo:} Connect rear BNC out to Solo Input 2 set to `Line', with clamp switch set to \textbf{Low}.
        \item \textit{MOTU M4:} Connect rear $1/4''$ TRS jacks to rear Line Inputs 3 \& 4 via TRS-to-TRS balanced cables, with clamp switch set to \textbf{High}.
        \item \textit{Siglent SDS804X HD:} Connect rear BNC jacks to Scope Ch 1 and Ch 2 via $50\,\Omega$ coaxial cables, with clamp switch set to \textbf{High} and scope vertical channel set to $1\,\text{M}\Omega$ input impedance.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
