\section{Executive Design Summary \& Scope}

This document establishes the electrical, mechanical, and PCB layout specifications for an optimized replica of the classic Pete Millett Soundcard Interface. The device serves as a precision analog front-end (AFE) attenuator, buffer, and galvanic isolation unit designed to protect computer audio interfaces while conducting laboratory-grade distortion (THD+N), frequency response, and noise floor measurements on audio equipment, including high-voltage vacuum tube amplifiers.

The design is customized specifically for tight bench integration with an M-Audio Solo audio interface situated at an operating distance of under $20\text{ cm}$. While the original Pete Millett topology relied entirely on through-hole components, discrete analog metering, and an active signal generator driver, this revision achieves substantial bill-of-materials (BOM) cost reduction, board space optimization, and signal integrity improvements through:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Architectural Pruning:} Total elimination of the legacy hardware generator output block and AD736JN-based true-RMS voltmeter. Modern measurement suites (REW, ARTA, Virtins Visual Analyser) handle excitation synthesis and calibrated digital signal tracking via software.
    \item \textbf{Active Silicon Upgrades:} Transition to Texas Instruments OPA1656 Next-Gen CMOS operational amplifiers, eliminating DC offset errors caused by input bias currents flowing through the high-impedance ($100\text{ k}\Omega$) divider.
    \item \textbf{Solid-State Overload Clamping:} Substitution of high-noise $50\text{ k}\Omega$ passive series protection resistors with depletion-mode MOSFET current limiters (Microchip LND150), reducing thermal Johnson noise voltage by a factor of four.
    \item \textbf{Impedance Calibration for Short Transmission Lines:} Implementation of a dual-footprint daisy-chain isolation resistor network, yielding a native $10\,\Omega$ output impedance for maximum voltage transfer into the M-Audio Solo without parasitic oscillation.
    \item \textbf{Modernized Power Infrastructure:} 6-pin power-only USB-C connector feeding an enhanced SMD crowbar overvoltage network and an XP Power JCA0305D03 metal-shielded isolated DC-DC converter.
\end{enumerate}

\section{DC Power Supply \& Input Protection Architecture}

The power delivery network converts an unregulated $+5\,\text{VDC}$ supply into low-noise, fully isolated $\pm 15\,\text{VDC}$ analog split rails. Because the interface is powered via standard external USB power adapters or host PC ports, robust primary-side protection is mandatory to safeguard against overvoltage transients and polarity reversals.

\subsection{Power Input Connector (USB-C 6-Pin)}
Legacy full-sized USB-B and coaxial barrel jacks are deprecated in favor of a modern 6-pin USB-C receptacle (Power Only).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Connector Pinout:} Provides two $V_{\text{BUS}}$ pins, two Ground pins, and two Configuration Channel (CC1/CC2) pins.
    \item \textbf{CC Logic Configuration:} To ensure universal enumeration with standard USB Type-C to Type-C smart power supplies (E-marked cables), CC1 and CC2 must each be terminated to ground through an independent $5.1\text{ k}\Omega \pm 1\%$ pulldown resistor.
    \item \textbf{Solderability:} The 6-pin layout provides wide ($> 0.8\text{ mm}$) pad spacing, permitting rapid, high-yield hand soldering compared to 24-pin high-speed data receptacles.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Crowbar Protection Subsystem}
To prevent catastrophic failure should the user connect an off-spec power source (e.g., $9\text{V}$, $12\text{V}$, or $19\text{V}$ laptop chargers), an explicit two-stage crowbar protection circuit is positioned upstream of all active regulators.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Series Overcurrent Limiter (F1):} Upgraded from the legacy radial leaded RUSBF110 to an 1812 surface-mount resettable PTC fuse ($I_{\text{hold}} = 1.1\text{ A}$, $I_{\text{trip}} = 2.2\text{ A}$, $V_{\text{max}} \ge 16\text{ V}$). The 1812 package provides low internal resistance ($< 0.1\,\Omega$) and planar thermal dissipation.
    \item \textbf{Shunt Clamping Diode (D9):} Replaces the axial 1.5KE9.1A with an SMD DO-214AB (SMC) Transient Voltage Suppressor (TVS), specifically the \textbf{SMCJ6.0A} (or SMCJ6.5A).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Reverse Standoff Voltage ($V_{\text{RWM}}$): $6.0\,\text{V}$.
        \item Breakdown Voltage ($V_{\text{BR}}$): $6.67\,\text{V}$ to $7.37\,\text{V}$.
        \item Peak Pulse Power Dissipation: $1500\,\text{W}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \textit{Engineering Justification:} The original $9.1\text{V}$ breakdown diode allows dangerous voltage excursions before conduction, stressing the DC-DC input stage. A $6.0\text{V}$ standoff diode clamps immediately above nominal $5\text{V}$, driving F1 into high-impedance latch-off within milliseconds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Isolated DC-DC Converter (XP Power JCA0305D03)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Key Characteristics:} $3\text{ W}$ continuous rating, nominal $+5\,\text{VDC}$ input ($4.5\,\text{V}$ to $9.0\,\text{V}$ operating window), fully regulated $\pm 15\,\text{VDC}$ dual output at $\pm 100\text{ mA}$.
    \item \textbf{Galvanic Isolation:} $1,500\,\text{VDC}$ baseline isolation breaks ground continuity between the computer sound card and floating device-under-test (DUT) circuits.
    \item \textbf{EMI/RFI Containment:} Housed in an integrated nickel-coated copper metal enclosure. The conductive shell acts as an intrinsic Faraday cage, trapping high-frequency magnetic and electric fields.
    \item \textbf{Switching Profile:} Fixed switching frequency at $300\text{ kHz}$. Because $300\text{ kHz}$ sits over an order of magnitude above the audible audio spectrum ($20\text{ Hz}$--$20\text{ kHz}$) and its sampling limits ($192\text{ kHz}$ A/D Nyquist at $96\text{ kHz}$), switching intermodulation artifacts are readily rejected.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bulk Storage and High-Frequency Filtering Network}
The legacy design specified $35\text{V}$-rated radial electrolytic capacitors indiscriminately across both the $5\text{V}$ and $15\text{V}$ domains, consuming excessive board area. The revised component sizing is structured as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Input Bulk Decoupling (C22):} Downrated from $35\text{V}$ to $10\text{V}$ or $16\text{V}$ ($330\,\mu\text{F}$, low-ESR radial or aluminum SMD). Dimensions reduce from a diameter of $10\text{ mm}$ to $6.3\text{ mm}$.
    \item \textbf{High-Frequency Input Snubber:} An 0805 $0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ X7R ceramic capacitor is routed directly across pins 22/23 ($V_{\text{IN}+}$) and pins 2/3 ($V_{\text{IN}-}$) of the JCA module. Electrolytic bulk capacitors exhibit significant inductive reactance at $300\text{ kHz}$; the local ceramic shunt captures inductive current spikes before they radiate across the input plane.
    \item \textbf{Output Rail Filtering (C7, C8, C13, C18):} Downrated from $35\text{V}$ to $25\text{V}$ ($330\,\mu\text{F}$ low-ESR). Sized for reliable headroom above the continuous $\pm 15\,\text{VDC}$ rail potential while minimizing footprint height.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analog Front-End (AFE) Signal Conditioning}

The input stage accepts single-ended or balanced voltages ranging from line-level audio ($< 1\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$) up to high-voltage tube amplifier plate swings ($> 100\,\text{V}_{\text{RMS}}$).

\subsection{High-Voltage DC Blocking Capacitors (C2, C3)}
DC blocking capacitors isolate the sensitive solid-state buffer from the high DC anode potentials encountered in vacuum tube circuits.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Capacitance Optimization:} Pete Millett originally specified $1.0\,\mu\text{F}$ $400\text{V}$ polyester dielectric blocks to extend sub-audible measurement performance down to $1.5\text{ Hz}$. For standard audio analysis ($20\text{ Hz}$--$20\text{ kHz}$), the input coupling values are downsized to $0.47\,\mu\text{F}$ ($400\text{VDC}$ working rating).
    \item \textbf{Cut-off Calculation:} Across the $100\text{ k}\Omega$ system input impedance, the high-pass $-3\text{ dB}$ corner frequency is given by:
    \begin{equation}
        f_c = \frac{1}{2 \pi R C} = \frac{1}{2 \pi \times 10^5 \times (0.47 \times 10^{-6})} \approx 3.38\,\text{Hz}
    \end{equation}
    At $20\text{ Hz}$, the magnitude attenuation is below $0.06\text{ dB}$, and phase shift is negligible.
    \item \textbf{Dielectric Selection:} Metallized Polypropylene (PP) Film (e.g., WIMA MKP4 or Panasonic ECQ-E). High-voltage Class II SMD ceramic capacitors (X7R, X5R) are strictly prohibited due to severe Voltage Coefficient of Capacitance (VCC) non-linearities, which inject harmonic distortion under high-voltage AC swings.
    \item \textbf{CAD Footprint Provisioning:} The PCB layout incorporates multi-pitch radial through-hole pads with concentric drill points spaced at $5.0\text{ mm}$, $7.5\text{ mm}$, $10.0\text{ mm}$, and $15.0\text{ mm}$, ensuring compatibility with various film capacitor form factors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Protection Clamping \& Low-Noise Current Limiting}
When an overvoltage transient enters the front-end, high currents must be prevented from damaging the op-amp input stages.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Depletion-Mode MOSFET Replacement (R2, R16):} The original circuit used $49.9\text{ k}\Omega$, $2\text{ W}$ carbon/metal film resistors to limit fault current into the protection diodes. These resistors introduce significant thermal Johnson noise:
    \begin{equation}
        v_n = \sqrt{4 k_B T R \Delta f} \approx 28.7\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}} \quad (\text{at } 300\,\text{K})
    \end{equation}
    This noise creates an elevated noise floor, obscuring micro-detail in low-distortion audio testing.
    
    \textbf{Modern Implementation:} The fixed $50\text{ k}\Omega$ passive resistors are replaced with a back-to-back series pair of \textbf{Microchip LND150} depletion-mode N-channel MOSFETs (SOT-89 or TO-92).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Topology:} Source pins are tied together; the Gates are tied directly to the common Source center node.
        \item \textit{Normal Audio Conduction:} Under normal operating voltages ($|V| < \pm 15\text{V}$), both channels remain fully enhanced, exhibiting an incremental resistance of only $\sim 1.5\text{ k}\Omega$ to $3\text{ k}\Omega$. Thermal noise voltage drops to $\sim 7.0\,\text{nV}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$—a four-fold ($12\text{ dB}$) improvement in front-end SNR.
        \item \textit{Overvoltage Limiting:} When a high-voltage spike occurs, the differential gate-source voltage pinches off the conduction channel, saturating the pass current to a fixed $1.5\text{ mA}$ to $3.0\text{ mA}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input Clamping Diodes:} The fault current is sunk into the power rails via high-speed silicon switching diodes. \textbf{1N4148W} (SOD-123 surface mount) parts are selected. Standard power rectifiers or Schottky diodes are avoided because their high junction capacitance ($> 30\text{ pF}$) forms an unintended low-pass filter with the attenuator ladder, rolling off high-frequency phase response. The 1N4148W maintains a junction capacitance under $4\text{ pF}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Precision Attenuator Ladder \& Rotary Switch Interface}
Attenuation is handled through a precision 4-range voltage divider network (R4 through R7) configured to yield stepped attenuation scales ($0\text{ dB}$, $-20\text{ dB}$, $-40\text{ dB}$, $-60\text{ dB}$).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Switch Topology Simplification:} With the elimination of the dual-rail analog metering circuit, the original multi-wafer 2-pole switch is simplified to a single-pole four-throw (1P4T) configuration.
    \item \textbf{Component Selection:} Standard Alpha SR2611F 1-Pole, 12-Position rotary switch. The included adjustable metal mechanical stop ring is locked at hole position 4, providing an exact, positive-stop 4-position action while avoiding custom low-volume multi-deck switch costs.
    \item \textbf{Wiring Harness:} The switch is wired off-board using 5 active conductors (1 common input lead and 4 range taps). These conductors are bundled and tightly twisted to maintain high common-mode magnetic rejection.
\end{itemize}

\textit{DC Offset Analysis:} Across the worst-case $100\text{ k}\Omega$ input resistance, a traditional bipolar op-amp (NE5532, $I_B \approx 72\text{ nA}$) develops an uncalibrated DC offset:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{offset}} = I_B \times R_{\text{in}} = 72 \times 10^{-9}\,\text{A} \times 100 \times 10^3\,\Omega = 7.2\,\text{mV}
\end{equation}
In contrast, the OPA1656 generates:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{offset}} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{A} \times 100 \times 10^3\,\Omega = 0.001\,\text{mV} \quad (1.0\,\mu\text{V})
\end{equation}
This negligible DC offset preserves high DC stability without loading the calibration system.

\subsection{Differential Subtraction \& Instrumentation Topology}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Dual Buffer Topology:} U4A buffers the positive input path ($V_{\text{INP}}$), while U4B buffers the input reference/shield path ($V_{\text{INN}}$).
    \item \textbf{Common-Mode Hum Cancellation:} Both channels feed into a downstream high-CMRR difference amplifier (Texas Instruments INA134 or equivalent difference stage U7). Any electromagnetic noise, mains hum ($60\text{ Hz}$ or $50\text{ Hz}$), or radio-frequency pickup coupled equally to the BNC center pin and outer shield appears identically at the inputs of U4A and U4B and is canceled out by U7.
    \item \textbf{Chassis Ground-Reference Switch (SW2):} SW2 allows the user to switch the input BNC shield between direct circuit ground and a floating configuration. When ground loops between the DUT, mains ground, and host soundcard corrupt the noise floor, opening SW2 breaks the loop while U4B maintains proper signal-path referencing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{DC Nulling Calibration Stage (R25, TP1, TP2)}
To achieve laboratory-grade measurement accuracy, remaining input offset voltages can be nulled using the on-board calibration trim:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Components:} A $100\text{ k}\Omega$ multi-turn cermet trimmer (R25) connected to the offset trim pins, paired with two surface-mount loop test points (TP1 and TP2).
    \item \textbf{Calibration Procedure:}
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
        \item Terminate the input BNC with a $50\,\Omega$ or $75\,\Omega$ shorting cap; leave the interface powered on for 15 minutes to reach thermal equilibrium.
        \item Connect a $4\frac{1}{2}$-digit or $6\frac{1}{2}$-digit digital multimeter (DMM) set to the DC millivolt range across TP1 and TP2.
        \item Adjust R25 until the measured potential is $0.00\,\text{mV} \pm 10\,\mu\text{V}$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\section{Oscillation Prevention, Output Stability \& Cable Interface}

The Texas Instruments OPA1656 is a wide-bandwidth operational amplifier ($f_{\text{unity}} = 53\text{ MHz}$). At these frequencies, parasitic board capacitances and load cable capacitances can erode amplifier phase margin, resulting in high-frequency ringing or sustained oscillation.

\subsection{Active Layout Stability Rules}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Direct Rail Decoupling:} High-frequency surface-mount ceramic bypass capacitors ($0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ X7R, 0805) are placed directly against Pin 8 ($V+$) and Pin 4 ($V-$). Power traces route through the capacitor solder pad prior to entering the IC lead frame. A $10\,\mu\text{F}$ tantalum or low-ESR electrolytic capacitor is placed within $10\text{ mm}$ upstream on each rail.
    \item \textbf{Feedback Phase Compensation ($C_{\text{fb}}$):} An 0805 capacitor footprint is routed in parallel with the main feedback resistor ($R_{\text{fb}}$). This footprint is populated with a $10\text{ pF}$ to $22\text{ pF}$ C0G/NP0 dielectric ceramic capacitor. This small feedback capacitance introduces a high-frequency zero in the loop transmission, providing phase lead to stabilize the internal pole against parasitic input capacitance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Output Isolation Resistor (\texorpdfstring{$R_{\text{iso}}$}{R\_iso}) Analysis for M-Audio Solo}
Driving capacitive loads directly from low-impedance op-amp outputs degrades phase margin according to:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{pole}} = \frac{1}{2 \pi R_o C_{\text{load}}}
\end{equation}
Pete Millett's baseline design specified a conservative $47\,\Omega$ series output isolation resistor, engineered to drive up to 5 meters of audio cabling ($C_{\text{cable}} \approx 500\text{ pF}$ to $1000\text{ pF}$).

\subsubsection{Short-Cable Bench Condition (\texorpdfstring{$< 20\text{ cm}$}{Under 20 cm})}
For the dedicated M-Audio Solo installation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cable Length: $\le 20\text{ cm}$ shielded unbalanced patch lead.
    \item Total Parasitic Capacitance: $C_{\text{cable}} \le 25\text{ pF}$ to $30\text{ pF}$.
    \item Instrument/Line Input Impedance of M-Audio Solo: $\sim 10\text{ k}\Omega$ (Line) / $\sim 1\text{ M}\Omega$ (Instrument).
\end{itemize}

\textit{Evaluation of $10\,\Omega$ vs. $47\,\Omega$:} With only $30\text{ pF}$ of load capacitance, the secondary pole introduced by a $10\,\Omega$ resistor lies above $500\text{ MHz}$, well outside the amplifier unity-gain crossover. Dropping $R_{\text{iso}}$ to $10\,\Omega$ minimizes voltage attenuation across the interface-to-soundcard junction, preserving absolute calibration accuracy for precision voltmeters and software analyzers.

\subsubsection{Mechanical Switch Prohibition \& Daisy-Chain Footprint Implementation}
Routing front-panel mechanical switches to toggle between $10\,\Omega$ and $47\,\Omega$ is prohibited. Running unshielded traces to a toggle switch adds several picofarads of stray capacitance and acts as an RF antenna, creating the exact instability the resistor is designed to prevent.

\textbf{PCB Design Solution (Series Daisy-Chain Network):}
A series dual-resistor footprint with a bridgeable jumper pad is placed directly adjacent to the op-amp output pin:

\begin{center}
\fbox{OPA1656 Out} $\longrightarrow$ [ $10\,\Omega$, 0805 ] $\longrightarrow$ \textbf{Node A} $\longrightarrow$ [ $36\,\Omega$, 0805 ] $\longrightarrow$ \fbox{Output Jack}
\end{center}
\begin{center}
\hspace*{3.5cm} $\vert$ \hspace{2.2cm} $\vert$ \\
\hspace*{3.5cm} $\text{--- [ SMT Solder Jumper ] ---}$
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Bench Configuration (M-Audio Solo):} Both the $10\,\Omega$ and $36\,\Omega$ resistors are populated. A solder blob or low-resistance zero-ohm jumper is bridged across the $36\,\Omega$ resistor pad, shunting it out. The circuit operates natively at $10\,\Omega$ nominal impedance.
    \item \textbf{Field Configuration (Long Cable Drive):} Should the interface be repurposed to drive long cables ($> 3\text{ m}$), the jumper bridge is opened with a soldering iron or scalpel. This activates the $36\,\Omega$ element in series with the $10\,\Omega$ resistor, establishing a $46\,\Omega$ protection buffer without requiring PCB modifications.
\end{itemize}

\section{Balanced Output Line Driver Provisioning (DRV135UA)}

Although the current M-Audio Solo hardware uses an unbalanced 1/4-inch input, the PCB layout provisions for a future balanced XLR output to support higher-end audio analyzers.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Silicon Optimization:} The legacy DIP-8 DRV134PA is superseded by the surface-mount \textbf{Texas Instruments DRV135UA} (SOIC-8).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item THD+N: $0.0005\%$ at $1\text{ kHz}$.
        \item Slew Rate: $15\,\text{V}/\mu\text{s}$.
        \item Package: Compact SOIC-8, reducing board real estate by over $60\%$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unpopulated Footprint Strategy:} The DRV135UA, its bypass capacitors, and the chassis-mount 3-pin XLR connector footprints are etched on the PCB but left unpopulated. The single-ended output from the INA134 difference stage is routed directly to the primary 1/4-inch phone jack. When an audio interface upgrade occurs, the DRV135UA and XLR jack can be populated to activate the differential balanced output.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary \& Next Steps}

This engineering specification establishes a low-noise, cost-effective test front-end matched to the M-Audio Solo. By removing legacy measurement circuits, upgrading to modern CMOS and depletion-mode solid-state devices, and implementing a configurable $10\,\Omega$ output stage, the interface delivers professional measurement performance in a compact bench footprint.

The immediate follow-on engineering tasks are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item Generate the complete schematic netlist in KiCad including all unified power, protection, attenuator, and buffer sheets.
    \item Finalize the mechanical drilling template for the Hammond extruded enclosure front/rear panels.
    \item Execute PCB routing following the strict 2-layer star-grounding and high-frequency decoupling layout guidelines outlined in Section 5.
\end{enumerate}
